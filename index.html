The rule

    The code, in short

    Getting out
    • Watch first.Spend ten minutes on the beach. Find the peak, the channel, the sets and the crowd.
    • Use the channel, or go wide.Paddle out where the waves aren't breaking, never through the peak.
    • Go behind riders.Paddle toward the whitewater, away from the shoulder the rider is heading for.
    • Hold on to your board.A ditched board on a leash is a weapon for whoever is behind you.
    In the lineup
    • Join at the edge.Sit at the side of the pack and take the back of the queue.
    • Wait your turn.Whoever has waited longest goes next and sits deepest.
    • Move between sets.Change spots in the lull, around the back or through the channel, and start at the back again.
    • Know your level.If the peak is beyond you, surf the inside or another spot.
    On a wave
    • Closest to the peak has priority.The surfer with the longest ride ahead owns the wave.
    • Never drop in.Look toward the peak before you go. If someone is up, pull back.
    • Don't snake.Paddling around someone to sit deeper steals their turn.
    • Share.Being able to catch every wave doesn't mean you should.
    Coming in and messing up
    • Missed it? Clear out.Get off the takeoff spot, toward the whitewater, and rejoin at the back.
    • Go in wide.Ride one in or paddle in away from the peak and the paddle-out lane.
    • Mind the rip and the rocks.Go sideways out of the current. Exit on sand, not over rocks or shallow reef.
    • Apologize.Everyone makes mistakes. A quick "sorry" goes a long way.

    Words you'll hear

    Peak
    The spot where a wave first starts to break.
    Shoulder
    The unbroken part of the wave ahead of the rider, where the ride is heading.
    Inside / deep
    Closer to the breaking part of the wave. The deepest surfer has priority.
    Lineup
    Where surfers sit and wait, just outside where waves break.
    Impact zone
    Where the waves land. Bad place to sit, worse place to paddle.
    Channel
    Deeper water where waves don't break. Often has a current flowing out.
    Set / lull
    A group of bigger waves, and the calm gap between groups.
    Drop in
    Taking off in front of someone already riding the wave.
    Snake
    Paddling around someone to steal the inside position.
    Caught inside
    Being shoreward of breaking waves, with sets landing on you.
    Duck dive / turtle roll
    Pushing a short board under a wave, or rolling a long board over and holding on.
    A-frame
    A peak that breaks both left and right, so two surfers can share it.
    Etiquette varies by spot and country. When in doubt, watch how the regulars do it, give way, and smile.
